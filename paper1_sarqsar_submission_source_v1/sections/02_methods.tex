\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Datasets and audited molecular identity}

The empirical audit used BACE, BBBP, ClinTox, and HIV for classification and ESOL and FreeSolv for regression \citep{wu2018moleculenet}. Source SMILES were parsed and canonicalized with RDKit \citep{rdkit2026}. Molecular identity was canonical isomeric SMILES. Consistent classification duplicates were collapsed, conflicting-label identities were excluded, and regression duplicates were aggregated by arithmetic mean. Primary analyses preserved disconnected components found in the source-faithful record. Table~\ref{tab:datasets} gives the closed accounting.

\subsection{Scaffold identity and acyclic semantics}

Bemis--Murcko scaffolds were recomputed with chirality excluded \citep{bemis1996properties}. The primary convention assigned all acyclic molecules to one explicit scaffold identity; the regression sensitivity assigned each acyclic molecule a molecule-specific identity. Every partition was required to assign each molecule exactly once and to have zero train--test scaffold overlap.

\subsection{Empirical exact-size paired audit}

For each of 20 pre-specified partition seeds, a fixed-budget target-blind scaffold candidate pool was generated. The size-matched baseline minimized deviation from the requested 20\% test size without endpoint information. Response-aware selection was then restricted to candidates with exactly the same realized test size and minimized the absolute train--test endpoint-mean gap. Candidate generation, test cardinality, scaffold rule, search budget, and partition seed were thus controlled at the protocol level, but molecular and scaffold composition were not.

The primary audit used 300 requested draws per seed for classification, 20,000 for ESOL and FreeSolv under the shared-acyclic convention, and 5,000 for the singleton regression sensitivity. The shared-acyclic regression search had not converged by its frozen 20,000-draw cap and is not described as converged.

All models used radius-2, 2048-bit Morgan fingerprints \citep{rogers2010extended}. Classification used logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost; regression used ridge regression, random forest, and XGBoost \citep{pedregosa2011scikit,breiman2001random,chen2016xgboost}. Fixed configurations and production seed 17 were used. ROC--AUC was the primary classification metric and RMSE the primary regression metric.

The inferential unit was the unique paired partition, not a model seed. Each of 18 primary dataset--model cells contained 20 partition-pair effects, a 10,000-replicate paired-bootstrap interval for the mean, and a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Holm correction was applied across all 18 cells. A response-aware advantage required both adjusted $P<0.05$ and a bootstrap interval wholly above zero. RF/XGBoost model-seed repeats were robustness checks and did not increase inferential $n$.

For regression, the mean-only predictor assigned every test molecule the training-set endpoint mean. Its paired RMSE effect linked the observed empirical partition geometry to Equations~\ref{eq:mse-decomposition} and \ref{eq:mse-effect-decomposition}. Collateral diagnostics measured endpoint-distribution shape and scaffold composition but were not treated as controlled covariates.

\subsection{Molecular endpoint-permutation experiment}

The endpoint-permutation protocol was frozen before null outcomes were generated. For each dataset, \NullPermutationN{} independent permutations reassigned endpoint values across molecular rows. Molecular records, endpoint marginals, scaffold identities, group-size distributions, candidate-generation algorithm, nested search budgets, and the same \PartitionSeedN{} partition seeds remained fixed. The same endpoint permutation was reused across partition seeds, and the same regression permutation was reused across shared-acyclic and singleton modes.

For each permutation, size-matched and response-aware partitions were selected independently at every requested budget. Regression used the training mean as a constant test prediction and reported MSE, RMSE, MAE, and $R^2$, together with the exact decomposition in Equation~\ref{eq:mse-effect-decomposition}. Classification used the training prevalence as a constant probability and reported ROC--AUC, average precision, Brier score, and log loss. ROC--AUC was marked undefined rather than imputed when a test partition contained one class.

Seed-level effects were averaged within each endpoint permutation. The \NullPermutationN{} permutation-level means formed the null reference distribution. Partition seeds were not treated as independent replicates. We report the null mean, standard deviation, median, and central 2.5th--97.5th percentile interval. These percentile intervals describe the randomized reference distribution and are not confidence intervals for a superpopulation parameter. The null experiment was kept outside the empirical 18-cell multiplicity family.

\subsection{Exploratory empirical--null bridge}

After the pre-specified null experiment was completed, we added an explicitly exploratory bridge analysis. For each regression dataset and scaffold convention, the statistic was the mean mean-only RMSE effect across the 20 partition seeds. The empirical statistic was compared with the matching null distribution at the empirical search budget: 20,000 draws for shared-acyclic primary analyses and 5,000 for singleton sensitivities. One-sided Monte Carlo upper-tail probabilities used the plus-one correction, $(b+1)/(m+1)$, where $b$ is the number of randomized statistics at least as large as the empirical statistic and $m=200$ \citep{phipson2010permutation}. Because this bridge was formulated after the null results were available, it is labeled exploratory and is not used to redefine the primary empirical hypothesis family.

\subsection{Quality gates and reproducibility}

Hard gates required complete dataset--mode--seed--budget--permutation cells, exact paired test size, a response-aware target gap no larger than its baseline, regression MSE decomposition residual below $10^{-9}$, constant-score AUC invariance, and matching protocol, configuration, and clean-data hashes. The final run contained \RawSeedRows{} partition-seed rows and \PermutationRows{} permutation-level rows; the maximum absolute MSE decomposition residual was \MaxMSEResidual{}. Candidate caches and seed-level checkpoints were immutable under matching hashes and allowed interrupted runs to resume.
