\section{Results}

\subsection{The paired design fixed test size and froze search effort before model outcomes}

Every primary dataset produced 20 requested and 20 unique size-matched scaffold partitions and 20 requested and 20 unique target-mean-balanced counterparts. Within each partition seed, the paired protocols had exactly the same test-set cardinality: 303 molecules for BACE, 393 for BBBP, 288 for ClinTox, 8,224 for HIV, 223 for ESOL, and 128 for FreeSolv. No duplicate partition hash entered the inferential sample as an independent replicate. Complete integrity counts are reported in Supporting Information Table S4 and visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:budget-audit}.

Candidate-budget auditing showed that search effort was itself part of benchmark construction. Classification was frozen at 300 target-blind candidates. Under the primary single-group treatment of acyclic molecules, the mean selected target-mean gap for ESOL decreased from 0.0346 at 3,000 candidates to 0.00179 at 20,000, and FreeSolv decreased from 1.076 to 0.687 over the same range. Neither regression series met the pre-specified stopping criterion by the largest audited budget, so 20,000 was retained as a cap rather than interpreted as convergence. Figure~\ref{fig:audit-framework} summarizes the complete audit logic and Figure~\ref{fig:budget-audit} shows the frozen budget behavior.

\subsection{Classification provided no corrected evidence of a target-mean-balanced advantage}

Across BACE, BBBP, ClinTox, and HIV, none of the 12 classification dataset--model cells met the pre-specified joint criterion for a target-balanced advantage after correction across the 18-cell primary family (Figure~\ref{fig:primary-effects}; complete values in Supporting Information Table S5). Eleven of 12 mean AUC contrasts were negative, with HIV--RF the only positive mean contrast. Several unadjusted bootstrap intervals were displaced from zero, but the corrected decision rule remained inconclusive in every classification cell. The inference is therefore \emph{absence of corrected evidence for an advantage}, not evidence of equivalence.

\subsection{Primary regression effects were large but depended on scaffold semantics}

All six ESOL and FreeSolv regression cells met the corrected target-balanced-better criterion under the primary convention that all acyclic molecules share the \texttt{\_\_ACYCLIC\_\_} identity (Figure~\ref{fig:primary-effects}). Mean RMSE reductions were 0.105--0.293 for ESOL and 0.798--1.149 for FreeSolv. Complete primary statistics are reported in Supporting Information Table S5.

\begin{figure}[!p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure2_primary_effects_v3.pdf}
\caption{Primary paired effects across 18 dataset--model cells. Points show mean effects over 20 unique partition pairs and horizontal bars show paired-bootstrap 95\% intervals. Positive values favor target-mean-aware selection. Corrected inference follows the joint bootstrap-plus-Holm decision rule.}
\label{fig:primary-effects}

Changing only the semantics assigned to an empty Bemis--Murcko framework strongly attenuated this regression pattern. Under singleton acyclic identities, ESOL effects decreased to 0.036--0.061 RMSE units, while all three FreeSolv point estimates reversed sign; all FreeSolv bootstrap intervals crossed zero (Figure~\ref{fig:acyclic-sensitivity}; Supporting Information Table S6). The primary regression result is therefore conditional on the structural grouping semantics used to define scaffold novelty.

\subsection{Dominant-fragment representation changed point estimates but not corrected classification inference}

The structural audit identified multi-component source-faithful records in 105 BBBP molecules, 14 ClinTox molecules, and 3,086 HIV molecules. Deterministic dominant-fragment selection changed Bemis--Murcko scaffold identity for 5, 1, and 235 records, respectively, and produced full-record versus fragment Morgan similarity below 0.90 for 18, 5, and 640 records. Fragment mapping also created conflicting-label identities, confirming that single-fragment selection is not a lossless formatting operation (Supporting Information Table S9).

Across the nine affected dataset--model cells, all corrected dominant-fragment sensitivity inferences remained inconclusive, while seven of nine mean-effect directions reversed relative to the source-faithful primary representation (Figure~\ref{fig:fragment-sensitivity}; Supporting Information Table S10). Thus, inferential status was more stable than point-estimate direction.
