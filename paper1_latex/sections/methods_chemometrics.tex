\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Datasets and audited molecular identity}

Six public molecular-property datasets distributed through the MoleculeNet/DeepChem ecosystem were analysed: BACE, BBBP, ClinTox, and HIV for binary classification, and ESOL and FreeSolv for regression \citep{wu2018moleculenet}. Source SMILES were parsed and canonicalized with RDKit \citep{rdkit}. Invalid structures and non-numeric targets were excluded with row-level lineage. Molecular identity was defined by canonical isomeric SMILES. Consistent classification duplicates were collapsed; canonical identities carrying conflicting binary labels were excluded in full. Regression duplicates were aggregated by arithmetic mean while retaining source counts, ranges, and lineage. No duplicate canonical identity remained in the clean datasets. The closed raw-to-clean accounting, including duplicate and conflict rows, is reported in Supporting Information Table S1.

The primary analysis preserved disconnected components present in the source-faithful canonical records. A deterministic dominant-fragment representation was evaluated only as a pre-specified sensitivity perturbation.

\subsection{Scaffold identity and acyclic semantics}

Bemis--Murcko scaffolds were recomputed from the canonical representation with chirality excluded from the scaffold identifier \citep{bemis1996properties}. Every scaffold partition was required to assign each molecule exactly once and to have zero train--test scaffold overlap. Because an acyclic molecule has an empty Bemis--Murcko framework, the primary convention maps every empty framework to the explicit sentinel \texttt{\_\_ACYCLIC\_\_}, placing all acyclic molecules in one scaffold group. The regression sensitivity instead assigns each acyclic molecule a molecule-specific scaffold identity. These conventions encode different meanings of structural novelty and are analysed separately.

\subsection{Exact-size paired target-mean selection}

All primary paired scaffold partitions targeted 20\% of the cleaned dataset for testing. Let $G_1,\ldots,G_m$ denote complete scaffold groups and $n^*=\operatorname{round}(0.2N)$. For each pre-specified partition seed $s$, a fixed-budget target-blind candidate pool $\mathcal{C}_s$ was generated by random scaffold-group permutations. Groups were accumulated until the target size was crossed; removing the final group was allowed when it was at least as close to $n^*$. Duplicate scaffold subsets were removed, and target values were not used during candidate generation.

For candidate $c$ with test size $n_c$, size deviation was
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{size}}(c)=\frac{|n_c-n^*|}{\max(n^*,1)}.
 \label{eq:size-deviation}
\end{equation}
The target-blind size-matched baseline $c_s^{\mathrm{size}}$ minimized $d_{\mathrm{size}}$, with deterministic hash-based tie resolution. Target-aware selection was then restricted to
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{M}_s=\{c\in\mathcal{C}_s:n_c=n_{c_s^{\mathrm{size}}}\},
\end{equation}
so the paired candidate necessarily had the same test-set cardinality. Within $\mathcal{M}_s$, the selection criterion was the absolute train--test target-mean gap
\begin{equation}
 d_y(c)=\left|\bar y_{\mathrm{train}}(c)-\bar y_{\mathrm{test}}(c)\right|,
 \label{eq:target-gap}
\end{equation}
and the target-mean-balanced candidate was
\begin{equation}
 c_s^{\mathrm{bal}}=\arg\min_{c\in\mathcal{M}_s}d_y(c).
 \label{eq:paired-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus, the paired protocols share the dataset, partition seed, target-blind candidate pool, scaffold rule, candidate budget, and exact test size. They do not necessarily share the same molecular or scaffold composition; such collateral changes are measured explicitly and are not described as controlled covariates.

\subsection{Candidate-search budget and partition freezing}

Search budget was audited before predictive outcomes because a larger candidate pool provides more opportunities to find an extreme low-gap partition. A lower budget was considered stable only when its mean selected target-mean gap changed by no more than 0.02 target units and 5\% relative to every larger audited budget. Classification tasks were frozen at 300 candidates per partition seed. ESOL and FreeSolv under the primary single-group convention did not satisfy the stopping criterion by 20,000 candidates, so 20,000 was retained as a predeclared cap without a convergence claim. The singleton regression sensitivity used a separately frozen 5,000-candidate cap.

Twenty partition seeds were fixed before model fitting: 42, 123, 2024, 2026, 3407, 7, 19, 71, 101, 211, 307, 401, 503, 601, 701, 809, 907, 1009, 1201, and 1429. Every materialized partition was stored as a molecule-level manifest containing molecular identity, scaffold, target, assignment, and a SHA-256-derived partition identifier. Exact duplicate partition hashes were never treated as independent replicates.

\subsection{Predictive models, metrics, and inferential unit}

All models used the same radius-2, 2048-bit Morgan fingerprint \citep{rogers2010extended}. Classification models were logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), and XGBoost (XGB); regression models were ridge regression, RF, and XGB. Hyperparameters were fixed before production fitting and are reported in the reproducibility protocol and Supporting Information. Scikit-learn and XGBoost implementations were used \citep{pedregosa2011scikit,breiman2001random,chen2016xgboost}. Stochastic RF/XGB production fits used model seed 17 throughout; LR/ridge were deterministic under the fixed configuration.

ROC--AUC was the primary classification metric, with average precision, $F_1$, accuracy, balanced accuracy, and Brier score retained as supporting metrics. RMSE was the primary regression metric, with MAE and $R^2$ retained as supporting metrics. Supporting-metric effects are reported in Supporting Information Figure S4 rather than promoted to additional primary hypothesis families.

For classification, the paired primary effect for partition seed $s$ was
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_s^{\mathrm{class}}=\mathrm{AUC}_{s,\mathrm{bal}}-\mathrm{AUC}_{s,\mathrm{size}},
 \label{eq:class-effect}
\end{equation}
and for regression
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_s^{\mathrm{reg}}=\mathrm{RMSE}_{s,\mathrm{size}}-\mathrm{RMSE}_{s,\mathrm{bal}}.
 \label{eq:reg-effect}
\end{equation}
Positive $\Delta$ therefore favors target-mean-aware selection for both task types. The statistical unit was the unique paired partition ($n=20$ per dataset--model cell), not a model seed. Each of the 18 primary cells received a 10,000-replicate paired bootstrap interval for the mean and a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test; Holm correction was applied jointly across all 18 cells. A cell was labelled target-balanced better only when the Holm-adjusted $P<0.05$ and the bootstrap interval lay wholly above zero; the analogous rule below zero defined target-balanced worse. All other cells were labelled inconclusive.

\subsection{Collateral partition diagnostics and mean-only regression control}

For every size-matched and target-mean-balanced partition we recorded train/test target means, standard deviations, quantiles, two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistics, Wasserstein distances, test-set scaffold counts, largest- and top-five-scaffold fractions, scaffold HHI, effective scaffold number, Gini concentration, and acyclic fraction. These quantities were not included in the selection objective and are used to show which benchmark properties changed as consequences of target-aware partition selection.

Regression received an additional mechanistic control. For each paired partition, a mean-only predictor assigned every test molecule the training-set target mean. Its error obeys the identity
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{mean\text{-}only}}=\mathrm{Var}(y_{\mathrm{test}})+(\bar y_{\mathrm{test}}-\bar y_{\mathrm{train}})^2,
 \label{eq:mean-only-decomp}
\end{equation}
which makes explicit the direct coupling between the target-mean selection criterion and RMSE difficulty. Mean-only paired RMSE effects were summarized with the same 10,000-replicate bootstrap procedure, descriptively rather than as an additional primary multiplicity family.

\subsection{Predeclared sensitivity analyses}

For the acyclic-scaffold sensitivity, ESOL and FreeSolv were rerun under singleton acyclic identities using the same 20 partition seeds, exact-size paired logic, models, and frozen 5,000-candidate cap. These results are interpreted as protocol sensitivity and are not added to the 18-cell primary family.

For the disconnected-component sensitivity, BBBP, ClinTox, and HIV were deterministically mapped to the fragment ranked first by heavy-atom count, then carbon count, then canonical isomeric SMILES. Consistent duplicate fragment identities were collapsed and conflicting-label fragment identities were excluded. Splits were regenerated from scratch using 20 partition seeds and the frozen 300-candidate classification budget. The nine affected dataset--model cells formed a separate sensitivity family with paired bootstrap intervals, Wilcoxon tests, and Holm correction across those nine cells only.

Finally, stochastic-model sensitivity was predeclared for RF and XGB on partition seeds 42, 123, 2024, 2026, and 3407. Model seeds 29 and 43 were added to production seed 17 for the size-matched and target-mean-balanced protocols. These repeated fits quantify model stochasticity but never increase the partition-level inferential sample size.
