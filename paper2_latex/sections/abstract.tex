\begin{abstract}
Aggregate accuracy and marginal coverage can conceal outcome-class failure and uninformative uncertainty under molecular distribution shift. We conducted a two-stage, representation-controlled study. First, frozen ECFP predictors for BBBP, ClinTox, ESOL, and Lipophilicity were audited under repeated random, label-blind scaffold, and similarity-cluster splits using point-performance, calibration, applicability-domain, conformal, and composition-aware selective diagnostics. Random splitting was optimistic, while chemical similarity ranked risk inconsistently across endpoints. At nominal 90\% coverage, ClinTox marginal and shift-weighted sets achieved overall coverage near 0.90 but only 0.068--0.158 positive-class coverage. Mondrian calibration raised positive coverage to 0.926--0.940, but returned two-label sets for about 71\% of compounds. These failures motivated TAME, a transport-audited multi-view envelope evaluated under the frozen RACER-C4 protocol. Two label-free weighting views can expand a Mondrian set only after prespecified transport checks and consensus; failed audits trigger exact fallback. Baseline containment prevents lower same-row coverage and new confident singletons; the sealed external test asked whether weakest-class coverage improved at acceptable deferral cost. In six prespecified EPA endpoints across five fresh seeds, TAME increased mean minimum-class coverage by 1.3649 percentage points (hierarchical-bootstrap 95\% interval, 0.5827--2.0051) while Macro correct-singleton yield decreased 1.6067 points, inside a prespecified 5-point efficiency guardrail. Both approved views passed all 60 endpoint--seed audits; the diagnostic ECFP view failed all 60. TAME produced no empty sets or new singletons. This locked Tox21 evidence supports auditable uncertainty expansion, not arbitrary-shift coverage or universal superiority.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ADMET prediction; conformal prediction; applicability domain; covariate shift; Tox21; transport audit
